> **In short:** a real courier will never ask you to pay a small "redelivery" or "address update" fee through a link, share a bank OTP, install an app or "verify" a parcel over a video call. If a message says your parcel is held, don't click the link — check the tracking number yourself on the courier's official website or on [Track My Ship](/). If you've already paid or shared details, call the **1930** cyber crime helpline immediately.

## Why courier scams work

Most of us are waiting for a parcel at any given time, so a message saying "your parcel could not be delivered" sounds believable. Scammers send these messages in bulk, copy the names and logos of well-known couriers and India Post, and add urgency: "your parcel will be returned in 24 hours". The aim is to get you to click before you think.

## The most common courier scams in India

### 1. "Incomplete address" SMS with a link

A text claims to be from India Post or a courier and says your parcel is held because the address is incomplete. A link leads to a page that looks official and asks for your address, then a small fee — often under ₹50 — by card or UPI. The real goal is your card details or UPI PIN, which are then used for much larger payments.

**How to spot it:** the link isn't the courier's official website (look closely at the spelling), the message doesn't give a tracking number you recognise, and you're asked to pay to "release" a parcel.

### 2. "Your parcel contains illegal items" call

Someone calls claiming to be from a courier, customs or the police. They say a parcel in your name, often an international one, contains drugs, fake passports or other illegal items. You're "transferred" to a fake police officer, sometimes on a video call with a uniform and an office in the background, and told you're under investigation or "digital arrest" unless you transfer money to "verify" your accounts.

**How to spot it:** police and other agencies don't arrest anyone over a video call, and they never ask you to transfer money to clear your name. There is no such thing as a "digital arrest". Hang up.

### 3. Delivery OTP and "cancel your order" calls

A caller claiming to be a delivery person or customer support asks you to read out an OTP so they can "reschedule" or "cancel" a delivery. The OTP is actually for a bank transaction, a UPI registration or a change to one of your accounts.

**How to spot it:** a real delivery OTP is shared face to face, only when the delivery person is standing with your parcel. Read every OTP message in full; if it mentions a payment, a bank or an account change, it is not a delivery OTP.

### 4. A cash-on-delivery parcel you never ordered

A parcel arrives with your name and address, marked cash on delivery. It contains something cheap or nothing useful. Sometimes your details were bought from a leaked list.

**How to spot it:** check your orders before paying. You can refuse a COD parcel you didn't order — it will simply be returned. See our [cash on delivery guide](/blog/cash-on-delivery-tips).

### 5. Fake customer care numbers

Searching online for a courier's helpline can turn up fake numbers placed by scammers. The "agent" then asks for a small fee, remote-access app installation or your card details.

**How to spot it:** take customer care numbers only from the courier's own website, which we link on each courier page (for example [DTDC](/dtdc-tracking) or [Delhivery](/delhivery-tracking)). Never install a screen-sharing app because a caller asks you to.

## How to check if a delivery message is real

1. **Don't use the link or number in the message.** Open the courier's official website yourself, or enter the tracking number on [Track My Ship](/).
2. **Check that the tracking number exists** and matches a parcel you're expecting. Scam messages often have no number, or a made-up one that shows nothing.
3. **Check your orders** in the shopping apps you use. A real delivery problem usually shows up there too.
4. **Remember that couriers don't charge receivers small fees by link** for redelivery or address updates. Customs duty on international parcels is a separate case: it is paid through the official courier or India Post process, and you can confirm the amount with them directly.

## If you've already clicked, paid or shared details

- **Call 1930**, the National Cyber Crime Helpline, as soon as possible, or report it at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/). The sooner you report, the better the chance of stopping the money.
- **Call your bank** to block your card or UPI and dispute the transaction.
- **Change passwords** for any account whose details you entered, starting with email and banking.
- **Uninstall** any app a caller asked you to install.

You can also report a suspicious SMS, call or WhatsApp message at **Chakshu** on the government's [Sanchar Saathi](https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/) portal, which helps block numbers used for fraud.

## Quick checklist

| Real courier | Scammer |
|---|---|
| Gives a tracking number you can check yourself | Gives no number, or one that shows nothing |
| Asks for the delivery OTP only at your door, with the parcel | Asks for an OTP over the phone or by message |
| Collects COD only for an order you placed | Asks for small "release" or "address" fees by link |
| Customer care number on its official website | Number from an SMS, ad or random search result |
| Never threatens arrest | Says you're under investigation and must pay |

Track My Ship never sends SMS, WhatsApp messages or calls about parcels, and never asks for payment. We only show tracking information when you enter a number yourself.
