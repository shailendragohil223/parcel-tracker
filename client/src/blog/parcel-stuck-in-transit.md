## Why tracking stops on "In transit"

A parcel is scanned when it is handled: at pickup, when it reaches a hub, when it leaves, and when it goes out for delivery. Between those points it is on a truck, a train or a plane, and nothing new is recorded. So the tracking can look "stuck" when the parcel is actually moving.

How long the gap lasts depends mostly on distance. Within a city or state, the next scan often comes the same day or the next. Between distant cities, especially by road, a day or more without a scan is common.

## When a long gap is normal

- **Long road journeys** — for example, from one end of the country to the other.
- **Sundays and public holidays**, when fewer hubs and vehicles are working.
- **Festival seasons and big online sales**, when hubs handle far more parcels than usual.
- **Heavy rain, floods or road closures** on the route.
- **Remote areas**, where vehicles to the delivery branch don't run every day.

## When it may be a problem

- No new scan for **more than 48 hours** on an ordinary route, or well past the expected delivery date.
- The last scan is the **same hub for several days**.
- A scan mentions a **hold, a misroute or an exception** (wording varies, e.g. "misrouted", "shipment held", "delayed due to…").
- The tracking shows a **different destination city** from yours.

## What to do

1. **Check the latest scan again** on [Track My Ship](/) and note the last place and time. A new scan often appears after a hub processes its backlog.
2. **Make sure the courier is right.** If the tracking looks strange, confirm the courier and number with the seller or sender.
3. **Contact the courier** after about 48 hours without an update. Give the tracking number, the last scan location and your delivery PIN code, and ask them to trace the parcel. Note the complaint number.
4. **Contact the seller** if you bought it online. They booked the shipment and can escalate it with the courier, or arrange a replacement or refund if the parcel is lost.
5. **Keep your phone reachable.** A parcel that finally reaches your local branch may go [out for delivery](/blog/out-for-delivery-not-delivered) quickly.

## What not to worry about

An expected delivery date is an estimate, not a promise. A day or two of delay on a long route, or around a festival, usually resolves on its own. The [parcel tracking guide](/parcel-tracking-guide#statuses) explains what each status means.
