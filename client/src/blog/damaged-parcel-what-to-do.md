> **In short:** if a parcel looks damaged at the door, photograph it before opening and, if you can, mention the damage to the delivery person or refuse it. If you find damage after opening, record an unboxing video or photos straight away, keep all the packaging, and report it to the seller (for online orders) or the courier (if you sent it) as quickly as possible with the tracking number.

## At the door: check before you accept

Take a quick look before the delivery person leaves:

- Is the box **crushed, torn, wet or re-taped**?
- Does it **rattle** when it shouldn't, or feel lighter than expected?
- Is the **seal** on the packaging intact?

If something is clearly wrong, take photos of the parcel with the shipping label visible. Depending on the courier and the seller, you may be able to note the damage on the delivery record or refuse the parcel. A refused parcel is returned to the sender — see [what RTO means](/blog/rto-meaning-in-courier-tracking). For online orders, check the seller's policy first: some prefer you to accept and then raise a return.

## After opening: collect evidence

1. **Record the unboxing** if you're opening something valuable — a continuous video showing the sealed parcel, the label and the opening is the strongest evidence.
2. **Photograph everything**: the outer box from all sides, the label with the tracking number, the inner packaging and the damaged item.
3. **Keep all the packaging**, including the box and fillers. The seller or courier may ask to inspect it.
4. **Don't use the item** if you plan to return it.

## Who to contact

| Situation | Contact first | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You bought it online | The seller or shopping website | They booked the courier and handle returns, replacements and refunds. |
| You sent the parcel | The courier where you booked it | You are the courier's customer; claims depend on the courier's terms and any insurance or declared value. |
| Someone sent it to you | The sender | They booked it and can raise a claim with the courier. |

Report it **quickly**. Sellers and couriers usually have a short window for reporting damage, often counted from the delivery date shown in tracking. Quote the tracking number, describe the damage, attach photos and note the complaint number you're given.

## If it isn't resolved

If the seller or courier doesn't respond after reasonable time, you can register a complaint with the **National Consumer Helpline** by calling **1915** or online at [consumerhelpline.gov.in](https://consumerhelpline.gov.in/).

## Sending something fragile?

- Use a strong box with cushioning on all sides, so the item doesn't move when shaken.
- Mark it fragile, but don't rely on the label alone.
- Ask at booking about declaring the value or insurance for valuable items, and keep the receipt.
- Write a complete address so the parcel isn't handled more than necessary — see [how to write a delivery address in India](/blog/how-to-write-delivery-address-india).
