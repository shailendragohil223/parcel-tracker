> **In short:** use a strong, correctly sized box, wrap each item separately, fill every gap so nothing moves, and seal all seams with wide tape. Write the full address with PIN code and phone number on the box itself as well as on the label, keep the booking receipt, and declare the real value. Most damaged parcels were packed in a box that was too weak or too big.

## Why packing matters

A courier parcel isn't carried gently from your hands to the receiver's. It is loaded onto trucks, stacked under heavier parcels, moved on conveyor belts and sorted at several hubs, sometimes by hand and sometimes by machine. A well-packed parcel should survive a drop from waist height and a heavy box placed on top of it. Packing well is also your evidence if something goes wrong: couriers can refuse damage claims for parcels that weren't packed properly.

## Step 1: choose the right box

- **Use a new or nearly new corrugated cardboard box.** Old boxes lose strength each time they're used, especially if they've got damp.
- **Pick a size that leaves about 5 cm on every side** for padding. A box that's too big gets crushed; one that's too small leaves no room for padding.
- **Use double-wall boxes** for heavy or fragile items.
- **Avoid carry bags, sacks and gift wrap** as the outer layer. Labels tear off and contents get crushed.

## Step 2: wrap each item

- **Wrap fragile items one by one** in bubble wrap, foam or several layers of newspaper. Items shouldn't touch each other.
- **Protect corners and edges** of frames, electronics and books with extra padding or cardboard corners.
- **Seal liquids** in their own container, tape the cap and put them in a sealed plastic bag in case they leak. Check with the courier first: many limit what liquids they accept.
- **Use the original packaging** for electronics when you have it, inside an outer shipping box.
- **Keep small items together** in a pouch or smaller box so they don't get lost in the filling.

## Step 3: fill the gaps

Put padding on the bottom, place the items in the middle, then fill every gap on the sides and top. Use crumpled paper, bubble wrap or air pillows. Close the flaps and shake the box gently: if anything moves or rattles, add more filling.

## Step 4: seal it

- **Use wide packing tape (about 5 cm)** — not cello tape, masking tape or string.
- **Use the "H" method:** tape along the centre seam and across both edges, on the top and the bottom.
- **Remove or cover old labels and barcodes** on a reused box so the parcel isn't sent to an old address.

## Step 5: label it properly

- **Write the full address**, including the receiver's name, house number, street, landmark, area, city, state and PIN code. Our guide on [how to write a delivery address in India](/blog/how-to-write-delivery-address-india) has examples.
- **Add the receiver's phone number**, and an alternate number if possible. Delivery people call when they can't find the address.
- **Add your own address and phone number** as the sender, so the parcel can come back to you if it can't be delivered.
- **Write the addresses on the box itself** as well as on the label, in case the label comes off.
- **Mark it "Fragile" and "This way up"** if needed, but don't rely on it — packing matters more than stickers.

## Step 6: at the booking counter

- **Declare the real contents and value.** Couriers base any compensation on the declared value, and false declarations can mean the claim is refused.
- **Ask about insurance or risk cover** for valuable items. Some couriers offer it for an extra charge.
- **Check the receipt before you leave**: the receiver's address, PIN code, weight and tracking number should all be correct.
- **Keep the receipt** until the parcel is delivered, and send the tracking number to the receiver so they can follow it on [Track My Ship](/).

## Items couriers usually won't carry

Rules differ between couriers and change over time, but most refuse or restrict:

- flammable, explosive or pressurised items such as gas cylinders, fireworks, aerosols, petrol and some perfumes;
- cash, cheques and other negotiable instruments;
- illegal items, drugs and weapons;
- live animals and plants, and perishable food (unless the courier has a special service);
- lithium batteries sent on their own (batteries fitted inside a device are often allowed).

Ask the counter before booking anything unusual. If the courier finds a banned item, the parcel may be held or returned.

## Take photos before you seal the box

Take a photo of the items, of the packed box before sealing, and of the sealed and labelled parcel. If the parcel arrives damaged or something is missing, these photos make it much easier to show what you sent.
