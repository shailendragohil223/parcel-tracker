> **In short:** with cash on delivery (COD) you pay the delivery person when the parcel arrives, in cash or often by UPI. Keep the exact amount ready, check the parcel's label against your order before paying, and don't pay for a parcel you didn't order — you can refuse it and it goes back. If something is wrong after you've paid, raise it with the seller, not the courier.

## How cash on delivery works

When you choose COD on a shopping app or website, the seller books the courier with an instruction to collect a set amount from you. The delivery person collects that amount at your door, the courier sends it to the seller, and the seller marks the order as paid.

Three things follow from this:

- **The delivery person can't change the amount.** It's fixed by the seller when the order is placed. If the amount looks wrong, check the order in the app before paying.
- **The courier is only collecting money for the seller.** Returns, refunds and wrong items are handled by the seller.
- **If you refuse the parcel, it goes back to the seller** as a return to origin. See [what RTO means](/blog/rto-meaning-in-courier-tracking).

## Before the delivery day

1. **Track the parcel** on [Track My Ship](/) or the seller's app so you know when it is out for delivery.
2. **Keep the exact amount ready**, or check whether the order allows UPI or card payment at the door. Delivery people often don't carry much change.
3. **Make sure your phone is reachable.** Most delivery people call before arriving, and a missed call is one of the most common reasons COD parcels fail.
4. **If someone else will receive it**, give them the order details and the money, and tell them what to check.

## At the door: check before you pay

- **Your name and address** are on the label.
- **The seller's name** on the label matches the order you placed.
- **The amount** the delivery person asks for matches the order.
- **The package is intact**: no tears, re-taping or crushing. If it looks damaged, see [what to do with a damaged parcel](/blog/damaged-parcel-what-to-do) before accepting it.

Some sellers offer **open-box delivery**, where you can look at the item before the delivery person leaves. If yours does, check that it's the right item and in good condition. If not, you usually can't open the parcel before paying, so the label checks above matter.

## Paying by UPI at the door

Many couriers let you pay COD amounts by scanning a QR code on the delivery person's device or a payment link sent by the seller.

- **Scan only the QR code shown for that delivery** and check that the payee and amount on your UPI app match.
- **You only enter your UPI PIN to send money**, never to receive it. If anyone says you'll "receive a refund" by entering your PIN, it's a scam.
- **Keep the payment confirmation** until the order shows as paid in the seller's app.

## A COD parcel you didn't order

If a parcel arrives with your name and a COD amount, but you haven't ordered anything, **don't pay**. It may be a mistake, a gift sent by someone without telling you, or a scam that sends cheap items to people whose addresses have leaked. Check your orders and ask your family first. If nobody ordered it, refuse the parcel; it goes back to the sender. Our guide to [fake courier SMS and calls](/blog/fake-courier-sms-scams) covers related scams.

## If something is wrong after you've paid

- **Wrong, missing or damaged item:** report it in the seller's app as soon as you open the parcel. Take photos of the package and the item, ideally an unboxing video. Sellers often have a short time limit for these reports.
- **Paid but the order still shows unpaid:** share the payment proof with the seller.
- **Refund for a COD order:** sellers usually refund COD orders to a bank account, UPI ID or store wallet, since there's no card to refund.

## Is COD worth it?

COD is useful when you're buying from a seller for the first time or don't want to share card details. The downsides: you need cash or UPI ready at the right time, some sellers add a small COD fee, and a missed delivery is more likely to end in a return than with a prepaid order. For sellers you trust, paying online in advance often means a faster, smoother delivery.
